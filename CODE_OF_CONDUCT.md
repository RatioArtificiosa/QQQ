# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This project welcomes contributors regardless of experience level, identity, or
which language they think in. We are committed to a working environment where
technical disagreement is sharp and personal contempt is absent.

## The standard, stated concretely

**Expected:**

- Critique the code, the design, or the claim — and say why. "This is wrong
  because X" is the contribution; "this is wrong" is not.
- Assume the other person read the documentation, and that the documentation may
  be the thing that is wrong.
- Name your uncertainty. *"I could not verify this on macOS"* is more useful
  than a confident claim that turns out to be platform-specific.
- Accept a correction gracefully, and record it. This project's own
  `QQQ-Observations-and-Memories.md` exists partly to make admitting error
  ordinary rather than embarrassing.

**Not acceptable:**

- Harassment, insults, or sustained disruption.
- Personal or political attacks.
- Publishing others' private information without permission.
- Dismissing a concern because of who raised it.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, in any project
space, and when representing the project publicly.

## Reporting

Report a concern to the maintainers via the private channel described in
[`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), or by email if a public channel is the only option
available to you. Reports are handled confidentially. A maintainer involved in
the incident recuses themselves from handling it.

## Enforcement

| Response | When |
|---|---|
| A private word | A first, minor lapse — often a misread tone |
| A public correction | Behaviour that a reader would reasonably take as hostile |
| Temporary exclusion | A pattern, or a serious single incident |
| Permanent exclusion | Harassment, or retaliation against a reporter |

The response is proportionate to the behaviour and to whether it stopped.

## Attribution

This document is informed by the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/), rewritten to state
the standard in this project's own terms rather than adopting boilerplate that
nobody reads.

---

**A note on why this file exists at all.** A code of conduct is not a formality
for a project whose stated goal is to be trusted with other people's code. The
behaviour a project tolerates becomes the behaviour it attracts, and a project
whose maintainers cannot be disagreed with cannot be corrected.
